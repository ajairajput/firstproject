# Selling in Quincy, MA — Market Guide

_Compiled September 2026. Figures are current market snapshots, not guarantees — verify against a local agent's live comps before pricing._

## Market snapshot

- Single-family homes: **1.4 months of supply** — a seller's market. Homes sold at an average of **102.2% of list price**, averaging **22 days to offer**.
- Condos: **3.3 months of supply** — still seller-favored, but softer. Averaged **99.2% of list price**, **33 days to offer**.
- Single-family average sale price is trending up year-over-year (~$809k vs. ~$731k the prior year).

**What this means for you:** in the current single-family market, a well-presented home should move fast and near or above asking. The lever you control is *presentation* — condition and staging matter more than shaving days off timelines.

## Priority order for repairs

1. **Fix anything that could fail an inspection first** — roof, electrical panel, plumbing leaks, HVAC, foundation/moisture, radon. A gorgeous kitchen doesn't offset a failed inspection; these items also give buyers' inspectors less leverage to renegotiate price after you're under contract.
2. **High-ROI cosmetic/exterior projects** — garage door, front entry door, exterior paint, minor kitchen/bath refresh, hardwood floor refinishing. These recoup the highest percentage of cost and are the fastest to notice in photos and showings.
3. **Staging and cleaning, done last** — declutter, depersonalize, deep clean, stage the 3 rooms buyers weight most (living room, primary bedroom, kitchen), then get professional photos. Do this right before listing so it's not undone by ongoing repair work.

Budget guardrail: if your total renovation spend exceeds roughly **10–15% of the neighborhood's median sale price**, you're likely over-improving for the area — bring the home to neighborhood standard, not above it.

## Quincy-specific marketing angles

- **Proximity to the MBTA Red Line** — Quincy has multiple Red Line stops; commuter access to Boston is a major draw. Mention distance to the nearest station in the listing.
- **Coastal access** — Quincy's shoreline, beaches, and parks (Wollaston Beach, Merrymount Park, Squantum Point, etc.) are a differentiator versus inland South Shore towns. If you're within walking distance, say so explicitly.
- **Walkability to Quincy Center** — restaurants, shops, and the commuter rail/T access around Quincy Center add value for buyers who don't want a car-dependent commute.
- **School district and family appeal** — if relevant to your property, note proximity to schools; family buyers weight this heavily in MA suburbs.

## Staging checklist (the last mile before listing)

- Declutter every room to roughly a third of current volume; put keepsakes and excess furniture in storage.
- Depersonalize: remove family photos, memorabilia, and anything highly personal — buyers need to picture themselves living there.
- Repaint in light, neutral tones throughout (avoid bold/dark colors, especially in small rooms).
- Deep clean everything: carpets, windows (inside and out), grout lines, baseboards, light fixtures.
- Maximize light: clean windows, replace burnt-out bulbs, open blinds/curtains for showings.
- Stage the three rooms buyers weight most: living room, primary bedroom, kitchen.
- Curb appeal: mow, edge, mulch beds, clear walkways, pressure-wash siding/driveway, add a fresh doormat and simple potted plants by the entry.
- Hire a professional photographer — in a fast-moving market, listing photos are what earns the first showing.
